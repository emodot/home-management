Kitchen

One shopping list, still working at the market.

The whole house adds to the same list and sees it change live. At the market, check things off and price them even when the signal drops. Then the pantry, prices, gas, recipes and the week’s meals follow on their own.

Shopping list

Shop together, even offline.

Type an item and press Enter; everyone in the house sees it straight away. In shopping mode, check things off and enter what you paid. No signal? It keeps working, and sends the changes when you’re back, even if you close the app first.

  • One entry per item. Add tomatoes twice and you still have one entry for tomatoes, not two.
  • By store. Group the list by where you buy each thing: the market, the supermarket, the corner shop.
  • Finish shopping. Confirm the prices and the total paid, and the trip becomes one expense, every price is saved, and what you bought goes into the pantry.

Pantry

A pantry that refills the list.

Know what’s in the fridge, freezer, pantry and store room, and what to use soon. Give an item a low-stock level, and when it runs low it goes back on the shopping list by itself.

  • Quick to keep up. Step quantities up and down as you cook; shopping adds to it for you.
  • Units that add up. Bought a bag, keep it in kilos? Tell Lintel “1 bag = 50 kg” once and the pantry does the sums.

Prices

What things cost, trip to trip.

Every priced line from every trip is kept. See the latest price per unit, how it’s moved since last time, the cheapest store over the last 90 days and a chart by store.

  • Market measures. Derica, mudu, paint buckets, bags, crates, tubers, tins and sachets as well as kilos and litres; they convert where they can, so prices compare fairly.
  • Tidy items. Lintel spots items that look like the same thing, “Tomato” and “Tomatoes”, and merges them with their history.
  • Cooking gas. Log each refill; Lintel predicts the next one from how long your cylinders last and can remind you a few days before.

Recipes and meals

From recipe to pot to shopping list.

Keep the house’s recipes with photos, steps and ingredients. Scale any recipe to the servings you need, see whether the pantry can cover it and what a pot costs, and send what’s missing to the list.

  • Plan the week. Breakfast, lunch and dinner, Sunday to Saturday. Cook one pot and let it cover leftovers later in the week.
  • The week onto the list. Turn the plan into a shopping list, less what’s already in the pantry, merged with what’s on it.
  • Cooked it. Take what you used out of the pantry in one tap, with Undo; the week’s cost is weighed against the groceries budget.
